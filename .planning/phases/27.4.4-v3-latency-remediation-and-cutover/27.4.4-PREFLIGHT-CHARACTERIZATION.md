## Long-tail diagnosis (qwen p99 root cause from 27.4.3 Plan 05)

Categorization, ranked by evidence:

- [ ] **Reasoning explosion (`<think>` block bytes growing)** — RULED OUT. `reasoning_bytes_p95` is **0** for all candidates; qwen never emitted `<think>` blocks under the bake-off prompt.
- [ ] **max_tokens hitting (truncation forces re-attempt)** — RULED OUT. Zero `finish_reason: "length"` across 39 successful calls; qwen p99 tokens-out is **354** (well under any cap; default 4096 is far higher than observed).
- [ ] **Network / TTFB dominance** — PARTIAL. qwen TTFB p95 is **16.4s** which is high but does NOT explain a 264.9s gen duration p95.
- [x] **Generation-rate degradation (token rate collapses on tail events)** — CONFIRMED. qwen p50 gen=7.3s for ~321 tokens (45 tps steady) but p95 gen=264.9s for similar token counts → tps collapses to ~1.3 on tail events. NIM stream throttling, internal back-pressure, or contended GPU is the most likely cause. Same pattern in llama (p95=564.4s).
- [x] **Abort-signal failure** — CONFIRMED (secondary). nemotron's worst event was `gt-010 — call ERROR after 967876ms: Request was aborted` → 16 minutes despite a 60s `PER_CALL_TIMEOUT_MS`. The OpenAI SDK's `AbortController` plumbing does not interrupt NIM streaming responses promptly. Fortunately this is bounded by the per-call AbortController eventually firing.

**Root cause:** NIM long-tail is a generation-rate (tokens-per-second collapse) phenomenon, not a reasoning explosion or output-cap problem. D-06 max_tokens caps are NOT load-bearing for the long-tail; they remain useful as defensive ceilings against runaway emission but won't move the p95 needle.
